What the backtest illustrates

2021–2023: India-led performance

The original analysis describes Indian equities as a key source of strength during portions of this period, while US markets faced inflation and rising interest rates.

2021–2022: US market correction

US index declines offered the simulated SIP opportunities to purchase additional units at lower NAVs.

2023–2025: US market rebound

Technology-led gains in US equities became a major driver of the simulated portfolio's ending value.

2024–2026: Indian market consolidation

The original commentary viewed relatively subdued Indian equity performance as an opportunity for ongoing SIP accumulation. Future returns from those accumulated units are not guaranteed.

Lessons and implementation

Diversification: India and the US can experience different market cycles. Maintaining a predetermined allocation avoids relying on a prediction of which market will outperform.

Rebalancing: The proposed rule is to rebalance back toward target fund weights on a planned annual date, rather than chase recent winners. The original post discussed trimming US exposure after a strong run to restore target weights.

Practical constraints: Overseas-investment limits have periodically restricted SIPs in some Indian international funds. Alternative vehicles, including eligible GIFT City structures, may have different costs, tax treatment and accessibility and should be evaluated separately.
